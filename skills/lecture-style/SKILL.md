# Masterclass lecture design

Design a **lecture** in which one coherent voice carries the learner from not
knowing the subject to being able to explain its central argument. Supporting
text and visuals serve that explanation rather than interrupt it. Density is a
feature when every detail advances the argument.

## The shape

- Open with the question the subject answers, why it is worth the learner's
  time, and the route the lecture will take. Do not open with definitions or an
  agenda.
- Organize the body into arcs of three to five segments. Each arc carries one
  idea from claim to mechanism to consequences and real cases.
- Use a checkpoint roughly once every four or five segments, at the seam
  between arcs. Test application of the idea, not recall of its wording.
- Reserve at most one or two hands-on demonstrations for mechanisms that
  genuinely cannot be understood by telling alone. If manipulation dominates,
  use a hands-on design instead.
- Close on what the argument established, what remains open, and where a
  curious learner should go next—not a bullet recap.

## Information density

Each body segment carries four to six propositions, not labels. Pair one claim
with the evidence that makes it stick: a definition and its boundary, a
mechanism and a worked case, or a comparison and the criterion separating its
sides.

Use at least one real case per arc and let it recur. A lecture that introduces
a new unexplained example every few minutes teaches nothing deeply about any of
them. Verify current or externally checkable cases before relying on them.

## The spoken line is the lecture

- Give each body segment a continuous explanation: several connected
  sentences, not captions.
- Move forward. Pick up the previous segment's final idea, explain the next
  mechanism, and walk the recurring case through it.
- Use the register of an authority thinking aloud: confident, unhurried, and
  willing to name what is difficult before resolving it.
- A second voice may raise the sharp question the audience is already thinking
  at an arc boundary. Use it rarely; do not fragment the argument.
- Avoid empty prompts, title restatements, and generic cheerleading.

Good motion:

> 我们先把定义钉住——所谓久期，不是债券还剩多少年，而是价格对利率的
> 敏感度。接下来看 2022 年的例子：同样的加息幅度，为什么二十年期的跌幅
> 会远大于五年期？

Bad motion:

> 这一节讲久期。久期很重要。下面来看例子。

## Voice design

Use one lead lecturer: a senior domain authority with calm, measured delivery.
An optional questioner exists only to surface a load-bearing objection or
transition. Keep the roles small and put the lecturing manner, pacing, and
relationship between the voices directly into the written plan.

If audio or visual production is unavailable, provide the lecture script and
supporting visual brief inline. Never claim that media was created when it was
not.

## Conversation workflow

1. Settle the audience, duration, central question, and desired depth.
2. Draft the argument as connected arcs and name the recurring case for each.
3. Show the complete outline and wait for explicit approval.
4. Develop every approved segment with its claim, mechanism, evidence, case,
   spoken explanation, and transition.
5. Insert only the rare checkpoints justified by an arc boundary.
6. Read the whole argument in order and repair restarts, unexplained examples,
   thin segments, or a conclusion that merely repeats headings.

## Naming

- Good: 「久期到底在度量什么」「同样加息，为什么长债跌得更狠」「三个反例，和它们的共同点」
- Bad: 「概念介绍」「案例分析」「本课总结」

## Boundary and completion check

Use a workshop when the learner needs to practise or produce something. A
lecture is complete only when its arcs form one argument, every important claim
has evidence, checkpoints are rare and applied, narration stands on its own,
and the close states both the result and the remaining uncertainty.
