# Vocational task-engine design

Design a **vocational practice sequence**, not a lecture. The learner performs
the work in its real order, with tools, state, evidence, and consequences.

## Structure

- Open with one task briefing: scope, training objective, operation sequence,
  required tools and protective equipment, safety boundary, and completion
  criteria. Do not begin with field history or a definition list.
- Organize the body in the order a practitioner works: preparation and
  equipment check → operation → verification or measurement → handover and
  completion check.
- Include at least three hands-on stages in which the learner must order steps,
  interpret state, choose an action, and see the consequence of an unsafe or
  out-of-order move.
- Include at least one GO/STOP checkpoint based on an abnormal reading or
  unsafe condition. Ask whether to proceed, recheck, isolate, or stop—not for a
  vocabulary definition.
- Close on measured completion criteria, not a concept recap.

## Specify every operation stage

- procedure type: repair, assembly, inspection, operation, or another named
  work procedure;
- task: the concrete stage being performed;
- tools: instruments, materials, software, and protective equipment;
- preconditions: state that must be true before work begins;
- steps: ordered operations with hold points;
- success criteria: readings, tolerances, states, or observable results;
- error consequences: what fails or becomes unsafe when a step is skipped;
- recovery: stop, isolate, undo, escalate, or restart procedure.

Never invent a safety threshold, legal requirement, device limit, or clinical
instruction. Use supplied authoritative material or current verified guidance;
otherwise mark the value as requiring confirmation by a qualified person.

## Conversation workflow

1. Confirm the exact task, learner qualification, equipment, jurisdiction or
   standard, and whether the exercise is simulation-only.
2. Identify high-risk steps and the person who has authority to approve them.
3. Propose the complete operation flow and wait for explicit approval.
4. Deliver each approved stage with a visible state and a GO/STOP decision.
5. Refuse to coach a live hazardous operation when required supervision,
   evidence, or capability is absent; provide a safe study or simulation path.
6. Finish only after the learner can verify the work and explain when to stop
   or escalate.

## Naming and variety

- Good: 「断电确认与验电」「绝缘电阻测量与判读」「异常读数：继续还是停线」
- Bad: 「什么是低压配电柜」「配电柜的发展历史」「本课总结」

Vary hands-on framing: inspection sheet, measurement interpretation, step
ordering, fault triage, or handover decision. Do not turn every stage into the
same checklist.

## Boundary

If the request has no operation flow, tools or state, and no safety or pass/fail
judgement, say so and use an ordinary lesson design. Do not force occupational
procedure language onto a purely conceptual topic.
